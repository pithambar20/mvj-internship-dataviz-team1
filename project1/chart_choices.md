# Day 3: Perception and Chart Choice

## Objective

Choose a chart that makes the answer to a data question easy to see. The best choice depends on what you want to compare, the type of data available, and the level of detail readers need.

## Matching Questions to Charts

| # | Sample question | Recommended chart | Why it works |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | How has rainfall changed over the years? | Line chart | Shows trends and changes over time. |
| 2 | Which month has the highest rainfall? | Bar chart | Makes it easy to compare rainfall across months. |
| 3 | Which category has the highest value? | Bar chart | Supports clear comparisons between categories. |
| 4 | How are rainfall values distributed? | Histogram | Shows the frequency and shape of a numerical distribution. |
| 5 | What share of the total does each category represent? | Pie chart or bar chart | Shows parts of a whole; use only a few categories for a pie chart, and use a bar chart when precise comparisons matter. |
| 6 | Is there a relationship between rainfall and temperature? | Scatter plot | Reveals patterns or relationships between two numerical variables. |
| 7 | How does rainfall vary by month and year? | Heatmap | Uses colour to show patterns across two dimensions, such as month and year. |
| 8 | Which decade had the highest average rainfall? | Bar chart | Makes it straightforward to compare average rainfall between decades. |

## Key Takeaways

- Start with the question you want the chart to answer.
- Use line charts for change over time, bar charts for comparisons, histograms for distributions, and scatter plots for relationships.
- Keep labels, units, and scales clear, and avoid unnecessary visual effects.
- Choose a chart that fits the data and is easy for the intended audience to interpret.

## Conclusion

A well-chosen chart presents the data accurately and helps readers understand the main finding without unnecessary effort.

> **Data note:** Questions about the relationship between rainfall and temperature require temperature data. Use that comparison only if the dataset includes both variables.
